# Video 1: Hardware, Software & AI (Script + Remotion Animation Flow)

Draft for Dadarzz. Target length about 7 minutes. Voice: learning together, not teaching.

## Working title

**You Use Hardware, Software & AI Every Day. Here's How They Connect.**

Alternate: *Hardware vs Software vs AI: Different, But Actually the Same?*

## Script

The scene numbers match the animation flow further down. Bracketed text is on-screen action, not spoken.

### Scene 1: Hook (0:00 to 0:20)

> Right now, you're watching hardware run software that I built with AI. Hardware, software, and AI, all working together, in this one video. [flash chip, code window, AI sparkle as each word is said]
>
> Most people use all three before they even get out of bed. So what are they actually, and how do they connect? Let's figure it out.

### Scene 2: Intro (0:20 to 0:50)

> I'm Dadarzz, and I'm learning engineering by building things: software, computer hardware, and AI. And the more I learn, the more I realize these aren't three separate worlds. They're one system. [title card: Dadarzz + tagline]

### Scene 3: The three, in one sentence each (0:50 to 1:40)

> Quick definitions, no jargon.
>
> **Hardware** is the physical stuff: the chip, the screen, the camera, the battery. Things you can touch.
>
> **Software** is the instructions. It tells the hardware what to do. Without it, your phone is just a pretty piece of glass.
>
> **AI** is a special kind of software that learns patterns from data, instead of following only fixed rules. It's how your phone recognizes your face or guesses the next song you'll like.

### Scene 4: How they overlap (1:40 to 3:40)

> Here's the part that intrigued me the most. These three overlap, and that's what makes them one system.
>
> **AI is software.** It's code and math, just a special kind that learns patterns from data.
>
> **Software runs on hardware.** Every app, and even the biggest AI model, needs real chips to run.
>
> **Hardware is built for AI.** Some chips are designed just to run AI models faster.
>
> **They depend on each other.** Better hardware makes better AI possible, and better software gets more out of any hardware.
>
> So they aren't competing fields, they're layers of one system. And that's what engineering is: not just knowing one piece, but making all the pieces work together. [Venn diagram builds; the center lights up: "Engineering"]

### Scene 5: The proof, this video itself (3:40 to 5:30)

> And you're looking at the proof right now. Remember the first line of this video?
>
> This video was made with software: the animations are code. That code was generated by AI: I described each scene, AI wrote the animation, and I reviewed and fixed it. And it's playing on your own hardware, the phone or laptop in front of you right now.
>
> Software, generated by AI, running on hardware. Three layers, one video, all working together. That's engineering, and it's what I want to learn with you. [device frame appears; code, then an AI sparkle, then a chip glow stack around it]

### Scene 6: Why learn all three (5:30 to 6:30)

> So why is it worth learning all three?
>
> **One: you understand the world around you.** The things you use every day stop being magic.
>
> **Two: you can build complete things.** Not just one piece, but a whole product that senses, thinks, and acts.
>
> **Three: the lines are blurring.** Smart devices, robots, self-driving cars. The more these blend, the more it helps to understand the whole picture.
>
> You don't have to master everything at once. I certainly haven't. But knowing how they connect makes every step easier.

### Scene 7: Outro (6:30 to 7:00)

> So that's how hardware, software, and AI connect, and why engineering is about making all three work together.
>
> I'm Dadarzz, and on this channel I'll explain these concepts and share what I learn as I build. If you want to learn with me, hit subscribe. Next up: [next topic]. See you there!

## Remotion animation flow

### Project settings

- Resolution 1920x1080, 30 fps (a 7-minute video is about 12,600 frames).
- One main composition, with each scene as its own `<Sequence>` so you can edit and re-render scenes separately.
- Record the voiceover first, then set each Sequence's start frame and length from the real audio timing.
- Check the current Remotion docs for exact APIs, since versions change.

### Visual style (defined in `src/theme.ts` and `src/fonts.ts`)

- **Color per layer, always the same:** Hardware = teal, Software = blue, AI = violet. Where the three meet, and for the word "Engineering", use the brand orange. The viewer learns the code in the first minute and every later scene becomes easier to read.
- Headings in Space Grotesk, labels and code in JetBrains Mono.
- Dark navy background, simple flat shapes, big text. Don't over-animate.

### Reusable components to build first

| Component | What it does | Used in |
|---|---|---|
| `TitleCard` | Name and tagline entrance | Scenes 2, 7 |
| `LayerIcon` | Chip / code window / AI sparkle icons in the three colors | Scenes 1, 3, 4, 5 |
| `PhoneMock` | Device frame standing in for the viewer's own screen | Scene 5 |
| `LayerStack` | Three layers stacking up with labels | Scene 5 |
| `VennDiagram` | Three overlapping circles, center highlight | Scene 4 |
| `ReasonCard` | Numbered card sliding in | Scene 6 |
| `Captions` | Subtitle bar synced to the voiceover | All scenes |

### Scene-by-scene animation flow

Frames assume 30 fps. Adjust to your real voiceover timing.

| Scene | Time | Frames | Animation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Hook | 0:00 to 0:20 | 0 to 600 | Black screen, then chip, code window and AI sparkle pop in one by one on the spoken words (spring scale-in). Then all three slide together into a single glowing row. |
| 2 Intro | 0:20 to 0:50 | 600 to 1500 | `TitleCard`: "Dadarzz" fades up with a short underline wipe, tagline types in below. Optional cut to a camera shot of you. |
| 3 Definitions | 0:50 to 1:40 | 1500 to 3000 | Three panels in turn. Hardware: chip assembles from pieces. Software: lines of code type on. AI: dots connect into a small network. Each gets its layer color and a one-line label. |
| 4 Overlap | 1:40 to 3:40 | 3000 to 6600 | `VennDiagram`: circles drift in and overlap. Arrows appear as you say "AI is software", "software runs on hardware", "hardware is built for AI". Center circle glows and the word "Engineering" appears. |
| 5 Proof | 3:40 to 5:30 | 6600 to 9900 | `PhoneMock` shows the viewer's own device. `LayerStack` builds around it line by line: code (software), an AI sparkle (generated by AI), then a glow on the device (your hardware). End with all three lit together. |
| 6 Why | 5:30 to 6:30 | 9900 to 11700 | Three `ReasonCard`s slide in on beats "one, two, three", each with a simple icon. |
| 7 Outro | 6:30 to 7:00 | 11700 to 12600 | Logo and name return, subscribe prompt, a teaser frame for the next video. |

### Animation rules that keep it clean

- Use `spring()` for pop-ins and `interpolate()` for fades and slides, driven by `useCurrentFrame()`.
- One idea on screen at a time. If a scene has more than three elements, stagger them.
- Cut to real footage (you building, screen recordings) between animated scenes so it feels like a vlog, not a slideshow.
- Keep motion under about 20 frames per entrance so nothing feels slow.

## AI prompt template (for generating each scene)

Paste this for every scene, filling in the brackets:

> I'm building a Remotion video (1920x1080, 30 fps, TypeScript/React). Read CLAUDE.md, src/theme.ts and src/fonts.ts first. Reuse my existing components: [list].
>
> Create a component for **Scene [N]: [name]**, [length in frames] frames long. It should: [describe the animation beat by beat with the spoken line next to each beat]. Use `spring()` and `interpolate()`, no external assets, and export it as a `<Sequence>`-ready component.

Tip: generate and review one scene at a time, fix timing, then move on. Do not ask for the whole video in one prompt.

## Suggested workflow

1. Finalize and record the voiceover.
2. Mark the timing of each beat in the audio.
3. Build the shared components and color system.
4. Generate scenes one by one with the prompt template, preview, and adjust timing.
5. Add real footage and captions.
6. Render and watch it once on a phone before uploading.
